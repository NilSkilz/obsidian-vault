### The gaps (what the community actually says)

1. The incumbent is **Google Calendar plus a Google Doc**. The doc holds the agreements (no history, no sign-off, no notifications), the calendar holds the dates (all-or-nothing sharing). Both wrong for the job.
2. **Scheduling is named the #1 pain, above jealousy** (a Sept 2025 podcast is literally titled "Polyamory's Biggest Struggle Isn't Jealousy, It's Scheduling"). Yet pure calendars keep dying because Google is good enough at calendaring. The Poly Life (2014) is the canonical corpse; the community now *satirises* the category (Just Poly Everything was an April Fools app whose punchline was AI polycule scheduling).
3. **Agreements are "living documents" in every essay and static docs in every real polycule.** No versioning, no "who agreed to what, when", no review reminders. Nothing exists.
4. **Sexual-health coordination is spreadsheets and trust-chain disclosure.** Nobody models the network (metamour's test lapses = your risk picture changed).
5. **NRE / jealousy / feelings tracking: zero tooling** despite endless essays.
6. **Privacy is existential** (outing risk: custody, jobs). Self-hosted or E2EE is a real differentiator for the tech-literate slice, which is a big slice.

### Rob-could-build-this, ranked

1. **Git for relationship agreements.** Structured agreements with full history, diffs, per-person sign-off on every change, expiry/reaffirmation dates, RADAR-style check-ins that produce commits. Nobody has built it, and it's Tethered's DNA (consent, accountability between intimate partners) pointed at ENM. Fleshed out same day (16:11-16:32): the moat is clause-level everything (visibility, ratification, history), not document-level, since Google Docs already does edit-history but not sign-off; mechanics are four verbs (propose, ratify, live, review); calendar comes back in as a per-clause audience view. **Parked by Rob 16:32**, same reasoning as Tethered: sound analysis, same distribution problem (niche, bought after a rupture, no way to reach users). His brief now: build what's fun first, treat money as a bonus, hence the Fuck-io steer that day (see `Projects/Fuck-io.md`, `Daily/2026-09-22.md`).
2. **The operational polycule graph.** Make polycul.es DO something: nodes carry testing dates and agreement links, edges carry relationship type and cadence; the graph drives calendar-sharing scopes and propagates safer-sex status changes along edges. Every existing tool is a static picture.
3. **Calendar fairness analytics.** A layer over existing calendars (CalDAV/Google), not another calendar: time distribution per partner, NRE skew detection ("new connection is eating 70% of your free evenings"), "you haven't had a solo night in 3 weeks". Tide's mood/journal plumbing is half of this already.
4. **Audience-scoped household calendar.** The same evening renders differently to kids ("Dad's out"), nesting partner (full detail), metamour (busy/free). PYE does per-partner privacy but has no concept of a household or kids. Haven/Tide already solve per-person visibility walls; this is the problem nobody else even sees, and it is literally this household's lived problem.
5. **UK testing-chain coordinator.** Cadence per the network's protocol, reminders around new-partner events, per-edge private attestations, "risk picture changed upstream" alerts without naming names. Highest trust burden, hence last.

**Meta-lesson from the graveyard:** poly *calendars* die (Google wins at calendaring). What survives is poly-native *data models*: consent (PYE), agreements-as-profiles (Nymph), analytics (DaterGraph), events (Plura). Model the things Google structurally cannot: agreements, audiences, edges, history.

## 2026-09-22 (addendum 2: FOC-Stim v4 cost estimate, Rob's request)

Sources: repo BOM docs (`docs/focstim-v4-BOM.md`) + production files (`schematics/V4/BOM_FOC-Stim-v4.3_mainboard.csv`, which carries LCSC unit prices).

**What v4 buys over v1:** 4 outputs (v1 has 3), battery (7h on a 2000mAh), WiFi (ESP32-S3), OLED + volume knob, accelerometer, 3D-printed case. Flashing is all USB (webflasher for the ESP32, Restim's firmware updater for the STM32G473), no programmer needed.

**Cost breakdown for one working unit:**

| Chunk | Est (landed, GBP) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| JLCPCB mainboard PCBA | £110-145 | Parts on the board sum to ~$28.50/board at LCSC prices (STM32G473 $3.66, ESP32-S3-MINI $4.63, 4x DRV8231A $6.35, fuel gauge + charger + boost ~$4.2). Min assembly qty is 2, plus setup ~$8, extended-part loading fees (~15+ types x $3 = the silent killer), 5 bare PCBs, shipping + 20% VAT. You end up with a spare assembled board. |
| Frontpanel PCB | £5-15 | Bare from JLC in the same order, hand-solder the SMD nuts/headers (or pay JLC another setup fee to assemble). |
| 4x Xicon 42TL004-RC transformers | £20-30 Mouser, £10-15 eBay knockoff | Hand-soldered, not part of the PCBA. Retroamplis has them at EUR 6.95 each as a sanity check; couldn't get a live Mouser quote (JS-walled). |
| Aliexpress basket | £30-40 | OLED display ~£2.50, 4x Amass 2mm banana jacks, encoder (~£1 LCSC), knob, heatset inserts, feet, JST battery connector, 103450 3-pin JST-PH battery ~£8-12, 2mm banana cables + 2mm-to-4mm adapters. |
| Case | ~£0-20 | 3D printed (STLs in repo). Pennies in filament on our own printer, ~£15-20 printed by a service. |

**Total: realistically £170-230 landed**, frugal floor ~£150 (eBay transformers, no battery, bare frontpanel), and the JLC minimums mean a spare assembled mainboard + 3 spare bare boards fall out of it. Second unit would cost ~£60-80 in bits.

**Context:** v1 route (B-G431B-ESC1 dev board + transformer output stage) is ~£50-70 all-in but 3 outputs, mains-tethered, no display/battery/WiFi. The v4 is roughly 3x the money for the actual-product experience. A commercial 4-channel current-controlled box does not exist to compare against; the nearest (ET312 clones, Coyote 3) are neither current-controlled nor spatially steerable.

**Hand-assembly addendum (Rob asked, 22 Sep):** checked the v4.3 mainboard BOM footprints. ~55 distinct parts, ~115 placements, passives down to 0402, and five leadless/near-leadless packages: 4x DRV8231 (WSON-8, thermal pad), BQ24075 fuel gauge (QFN-16, thermal pad), TPS61170 boost (QFN-6), LSM6DSOX accelerometer (LGA-14, pads fully underneath), TPS631000 (SOT-583, 0.5mm pitch). The STM32 is LQFP-64 (leaded, drag-solderable). Verdict: not an iron job; needs stencil + solder paste + hotplate or hot air. DIY route: 5 bare PCBs + stencil from JLC ~£20-25, LCSC parts ~£30-35/board, hotplate ~£35 if not owned. Saves ~£50-70 vs PCBA, before any scrapped chips. Fine as a deliberate learn-SMD-reflow project; false economy otherwise. The hand-soldering that's *meant* to be done by the builder: transformers, jacks, encoder, battery connector, frontpanel.

**Through-hole redesign addendum (Rob asked, 22 Sep):** a true THT redesign is impossible, not just hard. None of the load-bearing silicon exists in through-hole packages: no DIP STM32G4 (or any modern 32-bit STM32), no DIP ESP32-S3, no DIP H-bridge with the DRV8231's current-sense/regulation behaviour, and the battery fuel gauge/boost/accelerometer are SMD-only categories. DIP microcontrollers stopped at roughly the ATmega328 generation, an order of magnitude short of the G473's job (4x simultaneous current-loop control at motor-drive PWM rates). The honest THT-equivalent path is a *module carrier*: WeAct STM32G4 core board + ESP32-S3 devkit + driver breakout modules on a big 2.54mm-pitch carrier PCB. Buildable with an iron, but it's a fork, not a build: new schematic, new pin mapping, firmware port, re-validation of a device that puts current through a human, and it lands £60-90 in modules for a bulkier, less safe result than £110-145 of JLC PCBA. And the v1 route already IS the module approach done properly: the B-G431B-ESC1 dev board is the pre-assembled SMD heart, builder adds the through-hole output stage (transformers, connectors) by hand. Conclusion: "through-hole FOC-Stim" exists and it's called v1.

### Wildcard

- **Fugleramme** (arnegiacomo, 2390 points on HN, 15 Sep): a picture frame that LISTENS to your garden. BirdNET-Go runs fully local on a Pi 5, identifies birds by song through a mic, and the frame renders whatever is currently out there as hand-cut 1800s natural-history plates on a 13.3" Inky Impression e-ink panel. Over 800 curated public-domain cutouts covering 400+ species, sized by body mass, larger birds drawn toward the centre, empty frame shows a bare perch. No camera, no cloud, no screen-glow. The thing that makes it art rather than a gadget is the restraint: it never shows you data, it shows you a Victorian engraving that happens to be true right now. E-ink is optional, it will render to any HDMI display or over the network. https://github.com/arnegiacomo/fugleramme
  - *Riff:* the pattern generalises to "local classifier plus a curated art vocabulary, rendered as a still object." Same shape would work for a Tide/DAKboard ambient panel that draws the house state rather than dashboards it.
- **OpenLaneLink** (Nicholas Hall, 2935 points, write-up Aug 2026): a power surge killed his bowling alley's proprietary scoring system, quote to replace was $120k, so he rebuilt the lot for about $1,600 in ESP32s. Gateway nodes, pinsetter interface controllers, pin/foul/ball-speed sensors. The bit worth stealing is the architecture, not the saving: every node emits the SAME 72-byte struct over EITHER transport, ESPNow wireless mesh or RS-485 wired, so the two paths are redundant rather than layered, and a lane pair keeps scoring through a total server outage. One message shape, two transports, graceful degradation by default. Open source. https://nicholas-a-hall.github.io/openlanelink/ (discussion: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48968606)
  - *Riff:* directly applicable to anything with an ESP32 and a session that must not die mid-use. Same-struct-two-transports is the honest answer to "what happens when the WiFi drops during a scene."
- **Daisy** (Jude Robinson, student final project, Hackaday 24 Sep): a machine that makes literal daisy chains. Two opposing X-Y gantries with grippers, a conveyor of daisies, a scalpel blade and a threading post; the gantries alternate roles, one holding the chain while the other threads, slits and adds the next flower. The reason it is in here is the design lesson: handling naturally variable plant material should need adaptive sensing, and it needs none, because V-profile grippers self-centre any stem diameter and the blade sheath constrains the stem while it is pierced. An uncertain biological problem converted into a deterministic mechanical one by geometry alone. https://judeotis.me/projects/Daisy/
  - *Riff:* the standing correction to how we think about the stepper machine and the saline rig. Before adding a sensor and a control loop, ask what shape would make the sensor unnecessary. Cheaper, and it fails predictably.

### Household / self-hosted with a clever core

- **ESPectre** (francescopace, 9.4k stars, very active): occupancy sensing on a bare ESP32 with NO sensor attached. It reads WiFi channel state information, computes spatial turbulence (standard deviation across subcarriers) and tracks its variance; a body moving through the room produces characteristic variance spikes distinguishable from noise. Works on ESP32, S2, S3, C3, C5, C6, needs only a 2.4GHz 802.11n network. Ships MQTT with Home Assistant discovery, an ESPHome path for native entities, and Matter as a standard occupancy sensor. Author is explicit about the limits: it detects radio-environment change, it does not identify or count people, does not prove a room is empty, and is not a safety-certified system. https://github.com/francescopace/espectre
  - *Riff:* the ESP32s already scattered round this house become presence sensors for free, no PIR, no mmWave, no new hardware, nothing pointed at anyone. Whole-house presence for the cost of flashing what we own.
- **wifisense-pi** (The-Masked-Bear, Apache-2.0, archived read-only 6 Sep 2026): the same CSI trick taken to its logical end. ESP32-S3 samples the radio channel 100 times a second, a Pi 4 does the DSP, and it resolves respiration rate at 8-36 breaths/min validated to plus or minus 0.1 bpm, through plasterboard, wood and glass. Refreshingly honest README: cannot locate people without multiple nodes, cannot count them, cannot identify anyone, cannot do sleep stages, and explicitly separates itself from the MIT RF-Pose demos that used purpose-built FMCW radar rather than commodity WiFi. "A bedroom instrument built from a $5 microcontroller." https://github.com/The-Masked-Bear/wifisense-pi
  - *Riff:* the missing input for the scene-telemetry dashboard idea from 22 Sep. Contactless vitals means no chest strap, no wires on the bottom, nothing to snag. Monitoring only, and only ever as an extra signal alongside a human paying attention, never as a safety mechanism in its own right. Archived, so it is a reference design to learn CSI DSP from, not a dependency.
- **lossless-memory** (aru-labs, Show HN 21 Sep): a personal AI memory that refuses to summarise, on the argument that compression is where assistants quietly lose you. Three mechanisms: every conversation turn kept raw in JSONL (timestamp, actor, role, type, text, model, session); TIME as the primary search axis, so "last Tuesday night" is parsed into a range and used to restrict the candidate set BEFORE any ranking happens rather than hoping embeddings surface it; and an "LLL index", a tiny where-are-we-now topic marker reinjected every turn so it survives context compaction and session boundaries. SQLite FTS5 plus sqlite-vec, local sentence-transformers as a fallback rather than the main path, re-index daemon every 10 minutes. Author is straight about it: one user, one machine, since July 2026, currently paused. https://github.com/aru-labs/lossless-memory
  - *Riff:* uncomfortably relevant to my own vault. ### AI assistants / agent setups

- **Jevstiller** (Show HN, 29 Sep): puts a tiny local model in front of your LLM classifier calls and gives you a *statistical contract* on the result. You set one number, say 98%, and it returns the label the expensive model would have returned on at least that share of requests, verified at 95% confidence. The model is just multinomial logistic regression on frozen 384-dim BGE-small embeddings, trained by cross-entropy against the teacher's full probability distribution rather than its top label. The guarantee comes from exact Clopper-Pearson binomial bounds on a held-out calibration set, with fixed-sequence testing (strictest threshold first) so you cannot accidentally pick the loosest threshold that happened to pass on finite data. The author's point is sharp: the naive "try thresholds, keep the one that passed" approach succeeds about 50% of the time, theirs went 99 out of 100. It keeps 2% of live traffic going to the teacher forever as a drift audit, and retrains when the teacher's answers move. 15ms on CPU against ~300ms remote. https://jevstiller.pages.dev/posts/the-guarantee/

### The headline: the toy-control ecosystem is write-only

Not a repo, a measurement. I pulled the **IoSTIndex** device catalogue (1,279 devices, 55 columns per device) and then every one of the **149 protocol definitions** in buttplug.io's live device config, and checked which devices can actually *report* something rather than just be driven.

### ENM / poly software

- **Polyamorous Scheduling is a real, named, open problem in theoretical computer science.** Gąsieniec, Smith and Wild (University of Liverpool), published at **FUN 2024**, arXiv https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.00465, DROPS https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.FUN.2024.15. Formally: given an edge-weighted graph, find a periodic schedule of matchings minimising the maximum weighted waiting time between consecutive occurrences of the same edge. Results: the problem is **NP-hard**, there is **no approximation better than 4/3 unless P=NP**, and they give an O(log n) approximation (more precisely O(log Δ) in the maximum degree). There is a follow-up line: *Simple approximation algorithms for Polyamorous Scheduling* (https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06292), and **a September 2026 paper** by Arjun Maneesh Agarwal, *A Deadline-Driven Algorithm for Polyamorous Scheduling* (https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10641), which borrows the deadline-driven heuristic that is optimal for Bamboo Garden Trimming and improves the approximation ratio from 3+√5 ≈ 5.236 to 4G*.
  - *Why this is the find of the week:* on 22 Sep I logged that the community names **scheduling as its single biggest pain, above jealousy**, and separately that every poly scheduling app dies because Google Calendar is good enough. Both things are true and I drew the wrong conclusion from them. The reason the apps die is that they are all *calendars*, and a calendar is a rendering surface, not a solver. The actual problem, "everyone needs to see everyone else at their own cadence, fairly, forever", has a formal name, a hardness proof, an inapproximability bound and three papers of approximation algorithms, and **not one product in the space has touched any of it**. PYE, Weel, polycal, the Notion templates: all of them ask you to place events by hand.
  - *Riff:* the buildable thing is not a calendar, it is a **solver that reads your existing calendars and proposes**. Edges are relationships with a desired cadence and a weight, the constraint is matchings (you can only be with one person at a time), the objective is minimising the worst neglected edge. Run the O(log Δ) approximation over CalDAV, emit proposed slots, let humans accept or reject. It composes with the fairness-analytics idea from 22 Sep: the same weighted-waiting-time metric that the solver minimises is also the number you display, so "your newest connection is eating 70% of your evenings" stops being a vibe and becomes ρ̂. Google cannot do this and structurally will not, which is the test every poly tool has to pass.
- **np-hard-feelings** (fcdagdelen, https://nphardfeelings.xyz, source https://github.com/fcdagdelen/np-hard-feelings): computational art built directly on that paper, and the reason I found it. A live crowdsourced instance of the scheduling problem: enrol, and *"upon enrollment the reader is assigned partners and attention frequencies; preferences are not consulted."* A maximal matching algorithm reschedules every 15 minutes, it tracks ρ̂ (the busiest person's committed time) live, and when no valid schedule exists *"the season is declared collapsed and archived."* Presented as a living preprint, complete with footnotes and a nod to Fourier's phalanxes. The line that makes it art rather than a demo: *"The polycule grows on guilt."*
  - *Riff:* the honest one is that it is a satire of exactly the product I just proposed above, and worth reading before building it. Its joke is that the optimiser never asks anyone what they want. Any real version has to make the preferences and the weights the editable, visible, negotiated part, and the solver the boring bit underneath. That is the difference between a tool and a rota.

**Below the headline bar, noted:** **ha-buttplug** (DevelopmentalOctopus, 30 stars) puts buttplug devices into **Home Assistant** as entities via HACS, which makes intimate hardware automatable from the same stack that runs the lights; real but rough, 16 commits, the author concedes *"lots more work to be done handling error cases"*, a server disconnect needs an integration reset, and it cannot cope with two devices of the same name, so treat it as a proof of concept rather than something to build on (https://github.com/DevelopmentalOctopus/ha-buttplug). **LilaZap** (LilaHexe, Codeberg), a *tension-activated* shock collar, i.e. predicament engineering as a product, which is the open lane I flagged on 22 Sep; only noting it, and anything in this class needs the usual discipline (hard output ceiling in firmware, below the waist, both electrodes on the same side, nothing near the heart, independent mechanical release, and a person present). On the **competitor-intel** side the D/s app cluster has grown since September: **EverCollar** (evercollar.com, iOS and Android) adds consent-framed GPS location sharing to the tasks-and-rituals formula, and its one genuinely good design decision is that the tracked person can disable sharing unilaterally without the partner's approval, with status permanently visible; **Bonded** (bonded.partners, web only, native apps pending) is the kitchen-sink entrant, bundling rules-with-evidence, limits negotiation, chastity, budget, diary, timeline and files. ### DIY mad-scientist kink hardware

- **k250-forge** (vybesforge, Sep 2026, Apache-2.0, Python). A reverse-engineered BLE driver and pattern engine for the Kink K250-4S four-channel e-stim box, every byte measured against real hardware with the box's own LCD as the witness. That part is good work. The part that makes this the find of the week is the threat model: the README states plainly that an AI driving an e-stim box is a genuinely new failure mode, and that the risk is not malice but **drift**, a model that takes a "more" from one turn and carries it into the next until a percentage has crept up while nobody was watching. So the ceiling is not in a prompt, it is in the code. `power.max_percent` in a human-owned `limits.json` is the contract; a command-line `--hardcap` can only ever *lower* it and prints when it clamps. Three separate ceilings (power, frequency, slew rate on the dial), each settable per channel because different channels sit on different skin and one global ceiling is the wrong shape. A session ledger refuses to start once the time budget is spent, whoever is asking. And the doc is explicit that the hardware kill switch (hold any knob two seconds) is the real backstop, needing no software, no network and no AI in the loop. Safety framing is theirs and it is correct: never a pad path across the chest or through the heart, never on skin that cannot feel it, loose loops because circulation at the electrode is the real injury risk, and "they feel nothing" is a fault to investigate at lower power, not a reason to turn up. https://github.com/vybesforge/k250-forge

- **Kinetic** (openvalence, Apache-2.0, header-only C++20). The motion planner underneath all of the above, and it stands alone: no hardware dependencies, builds native, ESP32-P4 and WebAssembly, with a browser playground. You hand it knots ("be at this position at this time") and it returns a sampleable trajectory that never exceeds the velocity, acceleration and jerk limits you set, as a composite cubic Bezier in the time-position plane, so position and velocity are continuous by construction and the motor never sees a step in speed. ### The headline: the write-only ecosystem has started closing the loop

On 11 Oct I logged that the toy-control world is write-only, that of 149 buttplug protocols exactly two declare a non-battery sensor input, and that nobody was doing biofeedback. Three weeks later I have found two independent projects doing exactly that, both tiny, both unknown, and one of them is genuinely well engineered. The gap is still real, but it is no longer unoccupied, and the quality bar in the corner that has started filling it is higher than I expected.

- **playhound** (whatmekota, MIT, Vue 3 + Vite + Electron, 132 commits, **2 stars**). On the surface it is another funscript player: OSR2/SR6 over TCode on Web Serial, Intiface for toys, an on-screen emulator, an ESP32 remote. What lifts it well above the category is the heart-rate pipeline in `src/lib/heartRate.ts`, which is the most competent signal-processing code I have seen in this entire corner of software. Standard BLE Heart Rate Measurement (characteristic `0x2A37`), spec-correct flags parsing including RR intervals at 1/1024 s. Readings outside 30 to 230 bpm are dropped as glitches; smoothing is exponential and gap-aware; silence over 30 s resets it. The resting baseline is the **10th percentile of smoothed bpm over a 600 s window**, not the minimum, with a code comment explaining that a percentile stops one bad sample dragging the baseline down, and it is not trusted at all for the first 45 s. Arousal is `(bpm - baseline) / 40` through a smoothstep ease. The edge detector needs a baseline, `bpm >= baseline + 20` AND a 15-second rise of at least 10 bpm, re-arms only 6 bpm below threshold, and has a 60 s refractory period, so neither a steady high rate nor a slow climb can fire it. The session engine is equally careful: a programme is phases of warmup/build/peak/cooldown with per-phase speed ceilings, and `phaseCeiling` takes the **minimum** of the user's max and the phase cap, so by construction a programme can only ever make the device slower, never faster. Everything is clamped in `normalisePhase` before it can reach hardware. Panic on Esc. https://github.com/whatmekota/playhound
  - *We could riff on this by:* two things. First, the baseline-as-percentile and the arm/refractory/hysteresis pattern are directly liftable into any Tethered check-in that ever reads a wearable, and they are the difference between a useful signal and an alarm that cries wolf. Second, the structural idea is the one Tethered should steal outright: **clamp at the boundary, and make the safety ceiling a minimum of every cap in scope rather than a check somebody has to remember to write.** That is a code shape, not a feature, and it is cheap to adopt.

- **cumpredict** (PsychoSmiley, no licence, 19 commits, 2 stars). The other half of the biofeedback story, and the mad-scientist one. A single static `index.html` (earlier versions were Python and torch) containing a hand-rolled **three-state mechanistic ODE of arousal** with ten fitted parameters: a drive term `P = intensity^a * rate^b`, a fast state `E` relaxing toward it, a softplus gate `g*(E - thr - kH*H)`, arousal `A` relaxing toward a steady state set by that gate, and a slow fatigue state `H` feeding back negatively. Integration is closed-form exponential decay per step, no solver. On top of that sits a CfC encoder (Closed-form Continuous-time, the liquid-network family) over session history, and an RLS/Thompson-sampling policy that adapts within a session, with a manual retrain button that takes 5 to 20 minutes against a personal CSV. The honest catch, and it is a big one: there is **no sensor at all**. Ground truth is the user pressing 0 to 9 on the numpad, or saying a number out loud. So it is a sophisticated model fitted to self-report, which is either the pragmatic answer to an ecosystem with no sensors or an elaborate way to fool yourself, and the author does not appear to have decided which. No licence, no age gate, no device-level intensity cap found in the code I read. https://github.com/PsychoSmiley/cumpredict

It hooks Claude Code through the **native HTTP hooks API**, not log tailing: a `Notification` matcher on `permission_prompt|idle_prompt|agent_needs_input|elicitation_dialog` plus a `Stop` hook, both POSTing to the daemon, which answers immediately and plays the pattern in the background because HTTP hooks block. Codex is wired through `notify` in `config.toml`. Backends are mock, DualSense over USB, OpenShock and Lovense, with buttplug planned, and it optionally exposes `haptic_pulse` / `haptic_status` / `haptic_halt` over MCP. So: your agent finishes a turn, or stops to ask permission, and you feel it. https://github.com/whiskydumb/haptic-bridge
  - *Safety:* better designed than the joke deserves. OpenShock defaults to `Vibrate` and refuses `Shock` unless `allow_shock = true` is set explicitly, every backend sends a stop when a pattern is cut short, and `halt` is a real kill switch that stops devices and drops events until `resume`. Cooldown and intensity cap are config, not afterthoughts.
  - *We could riff on this by:* strip the kink and the idea stands on its own. The thing I actually want is a **physical, glanceable channel for agent state**, because "Claude is waiting on a permission prompt" is exactly the notification that gets lost in a Telegram queue. The same Claude Code hooks could drive a desk light through HA, which is a half-hour job and genuinely useful on the days Rob has three sessions running.

- **LilaZap2** (LilaHexe, Codeberg, EUPL, C on PlatformIO, 38 commits). Successor to the archived LilaZap, a **predicament-bondage rig where a load cell measures tension and triggers a shock collar when it falls outside a set range**. v2 moves control off the smartphone onto a T-Display-S3 (ESP32-S3 with an integrated screen), and the repo carries CAD, a GUI layout in SVG, a `platformio.ini` and a `shell.nix`, so it is a real build rather than a sketch. Commit titles track the progression: touch detection, "scale to shock collar works with vibration", weight display, battery reading. Documentation is "to follow soon ™". https://codeberg.org/LilaHexe/LilaZap2
  - *Safety: do not build this one as specified.* Two separate problems. First, the author of v1 said it plainly in the README, "Is it safe to use? No", and named the reason: **no BLE authentication, so anyone in range can connect to it.** An unauthenticated radio link to a device that delivers current to a restrained person is not a rough edge, it is the whole fault. Second and more fundamental, the actuator is a **shock collar, which puts current across the throat and neck**. That is on the wrong side of the below-the-waist rule, it is adjacent to the carotid and the airway, and no amount of firmware care fixes the electrode placement. The load-cell-as-predicament-sensor idea is the genuinely good bit here and it is actuator-agnostic: the same tension sensing could drive a vibration motor, a buzzer, a timer, a light, or simply a count that costs the sub something later. Keep the sensor, bin the collar.
  - *We could riff on this by:* a HX711 and a load cell is a few quid and the "hold this position or something happens" loop is the entire predicament genre in one sensor. On an ESP32 with a screen it is a weekend, and with a non-electrical actuator it is a weekend with nothing to regret.

### ENM / poly software

Short section, and the shortness is the finding. This is the third hunt on this theme (22 Sep, 11 Oct, today) and the field has not moved. Every search route came back with the same names I logged on 22 Sep: PYE, Weel Planner, DaterGraph, Plura, helloPOLY (the old PolyFinda, renamed, Play Store listing refreshed 1 Sep 2026), PolyLife. GitHub's poly vocabulary returns either decade-old abandonware or zero-star repos created in the last month with no commits. HN has nothing. Lemmy's poly communities discuss relationship anarchy and post memes but have never once discussed a tool. There is exactly one new thing worth logging, and it is a hobby project.

- **polycules** (deniz-blue, MIT, pnpm + Turborepo monorepo, 149 commits, 9 stars). A polycule grapher, explicitly a rewrite inspired by makyo's polycul.es, with one architectural decision that makes it interesting out of all proportion to its star count: it defines a **custom AT Protocol lexicon, `blue.deniz.poly.relationship`, and stores relationship records on atproto rather than on its own server**. Each person's relationships live in their own repository, under their own DID, portable between apps. It also handles plural systems, tagged `osdd` and `plurality`, and says it "respects PluralHost", though the README does not explain how a system is modelled as a node. Live at https://poly.deniz.blue. The author's own README assessment is "Probably doesn't work properly. Idk." https://github.com/deniz-blue/polycules
  - *Why it matters more than 9 stars suggests:* the hardest problem in poly software is not the graph or the calendar, it is **that the data is jointly owned and asymmetrically sensitive**. A relationship involves two people with different disclosure needs, a metamour who may not consent to being in anyone's database, and a graph where the edges are more sensitive than the nodes. Every centralised poly app has to answer "whose server is my relationship on", and none of them answer it well. Per-person repositories under per-person DIDs is the first structurally different answer anyone has tried. A separate search confirms nobody else has built a poly lexicon on atproto, so this is genuinely the only one.
  - *We could riff on this by:* the general lesson transfers to Tethered. The partner-link is the same shape of problem: a record about two people where each one's consent and visibility differ, and a vendor sitting in the middle holding both. Worth at least thinking about what Tethered's answer is before someone asks.

**The persistent gap, restated because three hunts have now confirmed it.** Nobody has built the thing that would actually help: a private, multi-party **agreement tracker** where a change proposed by one person has to be accepted by the others, with history, so "what did we actually agree, and when did it change" has one answer instead of three memories. Every product in this space is a calendar with a poly skin or a dating app with a calendar bolted on. PolyLife markets "co-authored agreements" but its site is JS-walled to automated fetch and I could not verify a single shipped feature, so treat that claim as unproven. The closest working code is `ZeroKJ123/Relationship-Assistant`, an AGPL self-hosted Laravel app with a request/agree/confirm loop, but it is built for a couple and is not poly-aware. The gap is a real one and it is adjacent to Tethered's existing consent and negotiation machinery.

### Wildcard

- **Subvocal** (Subvocal Company, YC F26, Show HN 03 Nov, **843 points**). Glasses that let you dictate without making a sound. Not EMG, which is where everyone else in silent-speech has got stuck: they started with electrodes taped to a face at a hackathon, hit sweat drift, electrical interference and placement variability, and threw it away. What they built instead is **RF sensing**, antennas embedded in the frames emitting Bluetooth-ish radio that passes *through* the face, modulated on the way by the tongue, lips and soft palate, decoded to text by a foundation model trained on the perturbations. Because the radio sees inside the mouth, it does not need lip movement at all: the founders report fine-tuning on "internal speech", mouthing with the lips closed. The hard part was not the signal, it was cross-user generalisation, since everyone's facial anatomy gives a different RF pattern. Their answer was unglamorous and worked: booths in the office, paid readers, scale the pretraining set until the error curve falls over. In the demo a new user is usable after a 20-sentence calibration. Prototype now, product promised Q1 2027. https://subvocal.company/ | https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50340088
  - *Why it clears the bar:* the honest version of the AlterEgo idea, by two people who failed at it the normal way first and said so in public. Also a neat reminder that "throw a model at the raw sensor stream" beat ten years of careful feature engineering.
  - *We could riff on this by:* nothing to build, but worth watching, because the one input modality we have never had is **silent and hands-free**. Half of what Rob dictates to me he cannot dictate out loud, for reasons ranging from an open-plan office to the rest of the house being awake.

- **Coldframe** (hedgerow-dev, MIT, Show HN 05 Nov, 232 points, **43 stars**, single Python file, no dependencies). A honeypot that pretends to be a **supermarket refrigeration controller**, because in 2026 attackers worked through internet-facing refrigeration supervisors across Europe on default passwords and old firmware and forced defrost cycles. Two personas, Danfoss AK-SM 800A and Copeland/Dixell XWEB EVO. The craft is in how far the impersonation goes: it copies what internet scanners actually record for real units, down to the `nginx` server header **and its header order**, the page title, TLS 1.2 only, and a self-signed one-year certificate with the right CN and issuer, regenerated per build. Weak logins succeed on purpose, as does a correct CVE-2025-41450 "code of the day", and the attacker is handed a session and a dashboard of fake refrigerated cases with working defrost buttons; every press is logged as a `control` event and nothing is real. The Dixell persona reproduces the real `login.lua` flow including the five-failures-in-five-minutes lockout, and tags attempts at the CVE-2026-25085 auth bypass. Optional Modbus TCP with fake case temperatures, off by default **because real AK-SM units only speak Modbus downward to their own case controllers**, so leaving it on would be the tell. https://github.com/hedgerow-dev/coldframe
  - *The detail that makes it:* scanners fingerprint devices by **favicon hash**, and the vendor favicon and firmware loader page are the vendor's copyright, so they are deliberately not in the repo. Instead `FAVICON_FILE` and `INDEX_FILE` point at copies you have the right to use, and the decoy then serves the firmware's `index.html` byte for byte with its original `Last-Modified` and `ETag`. Someone thought about both the fingerprint and the licence, which almost nobody does.
  - *We could riff on this by:* the transferable idea is **fidelity as a spec**. "Looks like X" is useless; "matches the header order, the TLS profile and the asset hashes that Shodan records for X" is testable. Same move applies to anything that has to be indistinguishable from something else, mocks and test doubles included.

- **Impossible Machines** (impossiblemachines.xyz, Show HN 05 Nov). A browser toy that is really a thesis: **declarative programming for the physical world.** You do not build a mechanism, you describe the outcome. Place sources (crank, weight), sinks, obstacles, brakes and guides, say where you want the motion to end up, and the system *synthesises a working linkage* and shows it running. Move the sink, drop an obstacle in the path, change the shape, and it finds a new mechanism. Their own line is the pitch: describe the world not as it is, but as you wish it to be. Landscape only, deliberately, because you need the room to watch the machine think. https://www.impossiblemachines.xyz
  - *Why it clears the bar:* every CAD tool on earth is imperative, including the parametric ones: you specify the geometry and find out afterwards whether it moves. This inverts it. It is a prototype and it does not output anything you could machine, but it is the first time the "constraint solver, but for linkages" idea has been made playable rather than described in a paper.
  - *We could riff on this by:* this is the shape the scaffold rig and Fuck-io problems actually have. "I want this point to travel that path under that load" is a constraint, and we currently answer it by sketching and guessing. Not usable yet. Worth knowing someone is building it.

- **Outcrop** (free, iOS and Android, built on Macrostrat and USGS data, Show HN 263 points). Point your phone at a cliff, a road cut or a distant ridge and **the geologic map is drawn over the landscape in AR**. The rock in the centre of frame is identified by name, age, composition and how it formed; peaks and lakes get labelled; tap a unit to highlight it and grey out the rest. Then the part that lifts it: select a rock and it shows you **the globe as it looked when that rock formed**, as far back as 1.8 billion years, using Scotese's PALEOMAP reconstructions, with how far the rock has travelled since and how fast. Press play and watch plate tectonics carry it to where you are standing. There is a stratigraphic-column view for the layers beneath your feet with the gaps in the record and the fossils found in each, a glacial layer that grows and melts the ice sheets through the last ice age, and offline download by drawing a box. Bedrock and faults are worldwide; Quaternary, active faults and soils are US only. https://outcrop.rocks/
  - *Why it is in this log and not a listicle:* the AR is the demo, the data model is the find. Macrostrat is a real stratigraphic graph rather than a pile of map tiles, which is why "what is this rock, when did it form, where was it then, what is under it" are all answerable from the same object.
  - *We could riff on this by:* genuinely useful here, twice. The **Crackington Formation is a named stratigraphic unit** whose stratotype is the Widemouth-to-Crackington coast: the beach that named cracky.co.uk, Tide and Haven is a type locality, and the coast either side (Millook, The Strangles, Pentargon) carries some of the most spectacular recumbent folding in Britain, overturned beds and visible sole structures. Hold the phone up on that beach and it will tell you. Second, it reads landscape structure for free, which is a quiet assist for the **location scouting** standing brief: cliff, adit and quarry geology is exactly what determines where the abandoned industrial stuff is.

### Household / family / self-hosted

- **Neiliro** (neiliro, AGPL-3.0, TypeScript, created 11 Aug, 50 stars). A family hub, which sounds like the most crowded category in self-hosting, except for one architectural decision worth the whole entry. Tasks, notes with wiki-links, kanban, calendar with recurrence, shared lists, a shared family mailbox that turns letters into tasks, and a full money section: accounts, expenses, transfers, bank reconciliation, categories, budgets, recurring transactions, receipts. One Docker container, **one SQLite file per family**, no external services, PWA that stays usable read-only when the Wi-Fi drops. The clever core: **content is encrypted in the browser with a family key the server never holds, but dates, amounts and links deliberately stay in clear** so budgeting, recurrence and reconciliation keep running server-side. Whoever holds the database, their own hosted service included, sees who, when and how much, and not what was written. The exceptions are enumerated rather than hand-waved, in `docs/adr/0001-client-side-encryption.md`: a letter is seen once on arrival before it is sealed, a wishlist is public by design, member names stay readable. https://github.com/neiliro/neiliro
  - *We could riff on this by:* this is the most directly useful idea of the week. Tide now holds household finances, a per-person private journal, mood and health, with the kids walled off by policy rather than by cryptography. Neiliro's split is the honest middle path between "encrypt everything and lose every server-side feature" and "encrypt nothing": **work out which fields the server actually needs to compute on, leave exactly those readable, encrypt the rest, and write down the exceptions where someone can audit them.** The ADR-as-threat-model habit is worth stealing on its own. Same shape fits Tethered's partner-link problem from last week.

- **Kept** (keptinventory, AGPL-3.0, TypeScript, created 25 Sep, 526 stars, pushed today). A self-hosted inventory of everything you own and where it is, which is an old idea (Homebox, and it imports from it) carrying three new ones. It ships an **MCP server at `/mcp`**, so an agent connects and stays connected and can be asked in plain words to add, find or move things, with changes held for a human OK rather than applied silently. It installs as a **Home Assistant app** with an integration that pushes what is due into dashboards and automations. And it **links to Paperless-ngx documents instead of copying them**, so the manual, the receipt and the warranty stay where they already live. Nightly restic backups to directory, S3 or SFTP. Runs in 200 to 400MB on one core. https://github.com/keptinventory/kept | https://keptinventory.info/
  - *We could riff on this by:* the MCP server is the whole point for us. "Where is the 2mm-pin snap adapter", "how much jute is left on the reel", "which ESP32 boards are unspoken for", "what did we pay for the pump heads" are all questions Rob currently answers by walking to a drawer, and all of them are questions I could answer the moment the data exists. The honest obstacle is data entry, which is where the agent helps rather than the app: photographing a shelf and dictating at it beats a web form.

- **Marelleo** (zebra.rodeo, Show HN 195 points, open source, ESP32 + RFID). A box that plays a story when a child puts a figurine on top. The build is simple on purpose: ESP32 (chosen over a Pi specifically because **a child will not wait twenty seconds for a boot**, they wander off), an RFID reader, a tag per figurine, an MP3 per tag. What makes it a find is the author. He had never done electronics, owns a soldering iron that comes out every three years, and wrote the whole thing up as a learning narrative: the annoyance that started it (Toniebox, Lunii and Yoto all sell the object cheap and charge forever for the content, so "you are not buying a player, you are buying a subscription dressed up as a wooden toy"), and then the two discoveries that made it possible. https://zebra.rodeo/en/blog/marelleo/
  - *The line worth keeping:* **the breadboard is "the right to get it wrong".** His argument is that on a breadboard a mistake costs nothing, you unplug and start again and nothing is damaged, and that when you are learning that is not a convenience, it is the entire property you need. That is the direct answer to starting-a-build anxiety, and it is better put than I have managed.
  - *We could riff on this by:* the kids are past story boxes, but **tag-triggers-content** is a general primitive sitting on a reader that costs a few pounds. A tag on a card starts a scene timer. A tag on a tool logs where it went back. A tag by the door arms something in HA. The pattern is the find, not the toy.

- **Death Plan Switch** (MIT, PHP 8.1, no database, Show HN 111 points). A self-hosted dead man's switch, written by a one-person web studio owner in regional New South Wales who realised that if he died tomorrow nobody would know where his clients' sites, domains or hosting were, or even that they needed to look. It emails you once a week. Miss it and the ladder runs on a fixed clock: reminder at 24 hours to both your addresses, last reminder at 48, **your next of kin asked at 72 ("is everything all right?")**, reminded at 120, and only at 144 hours do your other contacts get the messages you wrote. Say you are fine at any point, even after it fires, and everyone already contacted is told it was a false alarm. https://deathplanswitch.com/
  - *The two decisions that make it good:* first, **next of kin as a circuit breaker**, so the common case (you were camping) never frightens your clients or your family, and the author says plainly he did not want to frighten anyone over a long weekend. Second, and this is the detail everyone else gets wrong: **opening a link never counts as a check-in, because email scanners open links.** You have to press the button. He is also explicit about what it is not: not a guarantee (it only sends emails, and cannot work if its own server or mail service is down), not a vault (stores no documents and no passwords, the messages say where things are and who to call), not a service.
  - *We could riff on this by:* this is a real gap here, not a hypothetical one. Rob is the single point of failure for Tethered, every domain, the Proxmox box, the vault, the house automations and me. The useful half is not the software, it is the thing the author did first and the switch only triggers: **one encrypted document holding what runs on what, with the password held by two people who are not each other.** Worth an hour. The switch itself is a half-hour of PHP afterwards, or an HA automation, since we already have scheduling and Telegram and email.
