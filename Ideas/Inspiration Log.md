### The gaps (what the community actually says)

1. The incumbent is **Google Calendar plus a Google Doc**. The doc holds the agreements (no history, no sign-off, no notifications), the calendar holds the dates (all-or-nothing sharing). Both wrong for the job.
2. **Scheduling is named the #1 pain, above jealousy** (a Sept 2025 podcast is literally titled "Polyamory's Biggest Struggle Isn't Jealousy, It's Scheduling"). Yet pure calendars keep dying because Google is good enough at calendaring. The Poly Life (2014) is the canonical corpse; the community now *satirises* the category (Just Poly Everything was an April Fools app whose punchline was AI polycule scheduling).
3. **Agreements are "living documents" in every essay and static docs in every real polycule.** No versioning, no "who agreed to what, when", no review reminders. Nothing exists.
4. **Sexual-health coordination is spreadsheets and trust-chain disclosure.** Nobody models the network (metamour's test lapses = your risk picture changed).
5. **NRE / jealousy / feelings tracking: zero tooling** despite endless essays.
6. **Privacy is existential** (outing risk: custody, jobs). Self-hosted or E2EE is a real differentiator for the tech-literate slice, which is a big slice.

### Rob-could-build-this, ranked

1. **Git for relationship agreements.** Structured agreements with full history, diffs, per-person sign-off on every change, expiry/reaffirmation dates, RADAR-style check-ins that produce commits. Nobody has built it, and it's Tethered's DNA (consent, accountability between intimate partners) pointed at ENM. Fleshed out same day (16:11-16:32): the moat is clause-level everything (visibility, ratification, history), not document-level, since Google Docs already does edit-history but not sign-off; mechanics are four verbs (propose, ratify, live, review); calendar comes back in as a per-clause audience view. **Parked by Rob 16:32**, same reasoning as Tethered: sound analysis, same distribution problem (niche, bought after a rupture, no way to reach users). His brief now: build what's fun first, treat money as a bonus, hence the Fuck-io steer that day (see `Projects/Fuck-io.md`, `Daily/2026-09-22.md`).
2. **The operational polycule graph.** Make polycul.es DO something: nodes carry testing dates and agreement links, edges carry relationship type and cadence; the graph drives calendar-sharing scopes and propagates safer-sex status changes along edges. Every existing tool is a static picture.
3. **Calendar fairness analytics.** A layer over existing calendars (CalDAV/Google), not another calendar: time distribution per partner, NRE skew detection ("new connection is eating 70% of your free evenings"), "you haven't had a solo night in 3 weeks". Tide's mood/journal plumbing is half of this already.
4. **Audience-scoped household calendar.** The same evening renders differently to kids ("Dad's out"), nesting partner (full detail), metamour (busy/free). PYE does per-partner privacy but has no concept of a household or kids. Haven/Tide already solve per-person visibility walls; this is the problem nobody else even sees, and it is literally this household's lived problem.
5. **UK testing-chain coordinator.** Cadence per the network's protocol, reminders around new-partner events, per-edge private attestations, "risk picture changed upstream" alerts without naming names. Highest trust burden, hence last.

**Meta-lesson from the graveyard:** poly *calendars* die (Google wins at calendaring). What survives is poly-native *data models*: consent (PYE), agreements-as-profiles (Nymph), analytics (DaterGraph), events (Plura). Model the things Google structurally cannot: agreements, audiences, edges, history.

## 2026-09-22 (addendum 2: FOC-Stim v4 cost estimate, Rob's request)

Sources: repo BOM docs (`docs/focstim-v4-BOM.md`) + production files (`schematics/V4/BOM_FOC-Stim-v4.3_mainboard.csv`, which carries LCSC unit prices).

**What v4 buys over v1:** 4 outputs (v1 has 3), battery (7h on a 2000mAh), WiFi (ESP32-S3), OLED + volume knob, accelerometer, 3D-printed case. Flashing is all USB (webflasher for the ESP32, Restim's firmware updater for the STM32G473), no programmer needed.

**Cost breakdown for one working unit:**

| Chunk | Est (landed, GBP) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| JLCPCB mainboard PCBA | £110-145 | Parts on the board sum to ~$28.50/board at LCSC prices (STM32G473 $3.66, ESP32-S3-MINI $4.63, 4x DRV8231A $6.35, fuel gauge + charger + boost ~$4.2). Min assembly qty is 2, plus setup ~$8, extended-part loading fees (~15+ types x $3 = the silent killer), 5 bare PCBs, shipping + 20% VAT. You end up with a spare assembled board. |
| Frontpanel PCB | £5-15 | Bare from JLC in the same order, hand-solder the SMD nuts/headers (or pay JLC another setup fee to assemble). |
| 4x Xicon 42TL004-RC transformers | £20-30 Mouser, £10-15 eBay knockoff | Hand-soldered, not part of the PCBA. Retroamplis has them at EUR 6.95 each as a sanity check; couldn't get a live Mouser quote (JS-walled). |
| Aliexpress basket | £30-40 | OLED display ~£2.50, 4x Amass 2mm banana jacks, encoder (~£1 LCSC), knob, heatset inserts, feet, JST battery connector, 103450 3-pin JST-PH battery ~£8-12, 2mm banana cables + 2mm-to-4mm adapters. |
| Case | ~£0-20 | 3D printed (STLs in repo). Pennies in filament on our own printer, ~£15-20 printed by a service. |

**Total: realistically £170-230 landed**, frugal floor ~£150 (eBay transformers, no battery, bare frontpanel), and the JLC minimums mean a spare assembled mainboard + 3 spare bare boards fall out of it. Second unit would cost ~£60-80 in bits.

**Context:** v1 route (B-G431B-ESC1 dev board + transformer output stage) is ~£50-70 all-in but 3 outputs, mains-tethered, no display/battery/WiFi. The v4 is roughly 3x the money for the actual-product experience. A commercial 4-channel current-controlled box does not exist to compare against; the nearest (ET312 clones, Coyote 3) are neither current-controlled nor spatially steerable.

**Hand-assembly addendum (Rob asked, 22 Sep):** checked the v4.3 mainboard BOM footprints. ~55 distinct parts, ~115 placements, passives down to 0402, and five leadless/near-leadless packages: 4x DRV8231 (WSON-8, thermal pad), BQ24075 fuel gauge (QFN-16, thermal pad), TPS61170 boost (QFN-6), LSM6DSOX accelerometer (LGA-14, pads fully underneath), TPS631000 (SOT-583, 0.5mm pitch). The STM32 is LQFP-64 (leaded, drag-solderable). Verdict: not an iron job; needs stencil + solder paste + hotplate or hot air. DIY route: 5 bare PCBs + stencil from JLC ~£20-25, LCSC parts ~£30-35/board, hotplate ~£35 if not owned. Saves ~£50-70 vs PCBA, before any scrapped chips. Fine as a deliberate learn-SMD-reflow project; false economy otherwise. The hand-soldering that's *meant* to be done by the builder: transformers, jacks, encoder, battery connector, frontpanel.

**Through-hole redesign addendum (Rob asked, 22 Sep):** a true THT redesign is impossible, not just hard. None of the load-bearing silicon exists in through-hole packages: no DIP STM32G4 (or any modern 32-bit STM32), no DIP ESP32-S3, no DIP H-bridge with the DRV8231's current-sense/regulation behaviour, and the battery fuel gauge/boost/accelerometer are SMD-only categories. DIP microcontrollers stopped at roughly the ATmega328 generation, an order of magnitude short of the G473's job (4x simultaneous current-loop control at motor-drive PWM rates). The honest THT-equivalent path is a *module carrier*: WeAct STM32G4 core board + ESP32-S3 devkit + driver breakout modules on a big 2.54mm-pitch carrier PCB. Buildable with an iron, but it's a fork, not a build: new schematic, new pin mapping, firmware port, re-validation of a device that puts current through a human, and it lands £60-90 in modules for a bulkier, less safe result than £110-145 of JLC PCBA. And the v1 route already IS the module approach done properly: the B-G431B-ESC1 dev board is the pre-assembled SMD heart, builder adds the through-hole output stage (transformers, connectors) by hand. Conclusion: "through-hole FOC-Stim" exists and it's called v1.

### Wildcard

- **Fugleramme** (arnegiacomo, 2390 points on HN, 15 Sep): a picture frame that LISTENS to your garden. BirdNET-Go runs fully local on a Pi 5, identifies birds by song through a mic, and the frame renders whatever is currently out there as hand-cut 1800s natural-history plates on a 13.3" Inky Impression e-ink panel. Over 800 curated public-domain cutouts covering 400+ species, sized by body mass, larger birds drawn toward the centre, empty frame shows a bare perch. No camera, no cloud, no screen-glow. The thing that makes it art rather than a gadget is the restraint: it never shows you data, it shows you a Victorian engraving that happens to be true right now. E-ink is optional, it will render to any HDMI display or over the network. https://github.com/arnegiacomo/fugleramme
  - *Riff:* the pattern generalises to "local classifier plus a curated art vocabulary, rendered as a still object." Same shape would work for a Tide/DAKboard ambient panel that draws the house state rather than dashboards it.
- **OpenLaneLink** (Nicholas Hall, 2935 points, write-up Aug 2026): a power surge killed his bowling alley's proprietary scoring system, quote to replace was $120k, so he rebuilt the lot for about $1,600 in ESP32s. Gateway nodes, pinsetter interface controllers, pin/foul/ball-speed sensors. The bit worth stealing is the architecture, not the saving: every node emits the SAME 72-byte struct over EITHER transport, ESPNow wireless mesh or RS-485 wired, so the two paths are redundant rather than layered, and a lane pair keeps scoring through a total server outage. One message shape, two transports, graceful degradation by default. Open source. https://nicholas-a-hall.github.io/openlanelink/ (discussion: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48968606)
  - *Riff:* directly applicable to anything with an ESP32 and a session that must not die mid-use. Same-struct-two-transports is the honest answer to "what happens when the WiFi drops during a scene."
- **Daisy** (Jude Robinson, student final project, Hackaday 24 Sep): a machine that makes literal daisy chains. Two opposing X-Y gantries with grippers, a conveyor of daisies, a scalpel blade and a threading post; the gantries alternate roles, one holding the chain while the other threads, slits and adds the next flower. The reason it is in here is the design lesson: handling naturally variable plant material should need adaptive sensing, and it needs none, because V-profile grippers self-centre any stem diameter and the blade sheath constrains the stem while it is pierced. An uncertain biological problem converted into a deterministic mechanical one by geometry alone. https://judeotis.me/projects/Daisy/
  - *Riff:* the standing correction to how we think about the stepper machine and the saline rig. Before adding a sensor and a control loop, ask what shape would make the sensor unnecessary. Cheaper, and it fails predictably.

### Household / self-hosted with a clever core

- **ESPectre** (francescopace, 9.4k stars, very active): occupancy sensing on a bare ESP32 with NO sensor attached. It reads WiFi channel state information, computes spatial turbulence (standard deviation across subcarriers) and tracks its variance; a body moving through the room produces characteristic variance spikes distinguishable from noise. Works on ESP32, S2, S3, C3, C5, C6, needs only a 2.4GHz 802.11n network. Ships MQTT with Home Assistant discovery, an ESPHome path for native entities, and Matter as a standard occupancy sensor. Author is explicit about the limits: it detects radio-environment change, it does not identify or count people, does not prove a room is empty, and is not a safety-certified system. https://github.com/francescopace/espectre
  - *Riff:* the ESP32s already scattered round this house become presence sensors for free, no PIR, no mmWave, no new hardware, nothing pointed at anyone. Whole-house presence for the cost of flashing what we own.
- **wifisense-pi** (The-Masked-Bear, Apache-2.0, archived read-only 6 Sep 2026): the same CSI trick taken to its logical end. ESP32-S3 samples the radio channel 100 times a second, a Pi 4 does the DSP, and it resolves respiration rate at 8-36 breaths/min validated to plus or minus 0.1 bpm, through plasterboard, wood and glass. Refreshingly honest README: cannot locate people without multiple nodes, cannot count them, cannot identify anyone, cannot do sleep stages, and explicitly separates itself from the MIT RF-Pose demos that used purpose-built FMCW radar rather than commodity WiFi. "A bedroom instrument built from a $5 microcontroller." https://github.com/The-Masked-Bear/wifisense-pi
  - *Riff:* the missing input for the scene-telemetry dashboard idea from 22 Sep. Contactless vitals means no chest strap, no wires on the bottom, nothing to snag. Monitoring only, and only ever as an extra signal alongside a human paying attention, never as a safety mechanism in its own right. Archived, so it is a reference design to learn CSI DSP from, not a dependency.
- **lossless-memory** (aru-labs, Show HN 21 Sep): a personal AI memory that refuses to summarise, on the argument that compression is where assistants quietly lose you. Three mechanisms: every conversation turn kept raw in JSONL (timestamp, actor, role, type, text, model, session); TIME as the primary search axis, so "last Tuesday night" is parsed into a range and used to restrict the candidate set BEFORE any ranking happens rather than hoping embeddings surface it; and an "LLL index", a tiny where-are-we-now topic marker reinjected every turn so it survives context compaction and session boundaries. SQLite FTS5 plus sqlite-vec, local sentence-transformers as a fallback rather than the main path, re-index daemon every 10 minutes. Author is straight about it: one user, one machine, since July 2026, currently paused. https://github.com/aru-labs/lossless-memory
  - *Riff:* uncomfortably relevant to my own vault. ### AI assistants / agent setups

- **Jevstiller** (Show HN, 29 Sep): puts a tiny local model in front of your LLM classifier calls and gives you a *statistical contract* on the result. You set one number, say 98%, and it returns the label the expensive model would have returned on at least that share of requests, verified at 95% confidence. The model is just multinomial logistic regression on frozen 384-dim BGE-small embeddings, trained by cross-entropy against the teacher's full probability distribution rather than its top label. The guarantee comes from exact Clopper-Pearson binomial bounds on a held-out calibration set, with fixed-sequence testing (strictest threshold first) so you cannot accidentally pick the loosest threshold that happened to pass on finite data. The author's point is sharp: the naive "try thresholds, keep the one that passed" approach succeeds about 50% of the time, theirs went 99 out of 100. It keeps 2% of live traffic going to the teacher forever as a drift audit, and retrains when the teacher's answers move. 15ms on CPU against ~300ms remote. https://jevstiller.pages.dev/posts/the-guarantee/

### The headline: the toy-control ecosystem is write-only

Not a repo, a measurement. I pulled the **IoSTIndex** device catalogue (1,279 devices, 55 columns per device) and then every one of the **149 protocol definitions** in buttplug.io's live device config, and checked which devices can actually *report* something rather than just be driven.

### ENM / poly software

- **Polyamorous Scheduling is a real, named, open problem in theoretical computer science.** Gąsieniec, Smith and Wild (University of Liverpool), published at **FUN 2024**, arXiv https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.00465, DROPS https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.FUN.2024.15. Formally: given an edge-weighted graph, find a periodic schedule of matchings minimising the maximum weighted waiting time between consecutive occurrences of the same edge. Results: the problem is **NP-hard**, there is **no approximation better than 4/3 unless P=NP**, and they give an O(log n) approximation (more precisely O(log Δ) in the maximum degree). There is a follow-up line: *Simple approximation algorithms for Polyamorous Scheduling* (https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06292), and **a September 2026 paper** by Arjun Maneesh Agarwal, *A Deadline-Driven Algorithm for Polyamorous Scheduling* (https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10641), which borrows the deadline-driven heuristic that is optimal for Bamboo Garden Trimming and improves the approximation ratio from 3+√5 ≈ 5.236 to 4G*.
  - *Why this is the find of the week:* on 22 Sep I logged that the community names **scheduling as its single biggest pain, above jealousy**, and separately that every poly scheduling app dies because Google Calendar is good enough. Both things are true and I drew the wrong conclusion from them. The reason the apps die is that they are all *calendars*, and a calendar is a rendering surface, not a solver. The actual problem, "everyone needs to see everyone else at their own cadence, fairly, forever", has a formal name, a hardness proof, an inapproximability bound and three papers of approximation algorithms, and **not one product in the space has touched any of it**. PYE, Weel, polycal, the Notion templates: all of them ask you to place events by hand.
  - *Riff:* the buildable thing is not a calendar, it is a **solver that reads your existing calendars and proposes**. Edges are relationships with a desired cadence and a weight, the constraint is matchings (you can only be with one person at a time), the objective is minimising the worst neglected edge. Run the O(log Δ) approximation over CalDAV, emit proposed slots, let humans accept or reject. It composes with the fairness-analytics idea from 22 Sep: the same weighted-waiting-time metric that the solver minimises is also the number you display, so "your newest connection is eating 70% of your evenings" stops being a vibe and becomes ρ̂. Google cannot do this and structurally will not, which is the test every poly tool has to pass.
- **np-hard-feelings** (fcdagdelen, https://nphardfeelings.xyz, source https://github.com/fcdagdelen/np-hard-feelings): computational art built directly on that paper, and the reason I found it. A live crowdsourced instance of the scheduling problem: enrol, and *"upon enrollment the reader is assigned partners and attention frequencies; preferences are not consulted."* A maximal matching algorithm reschedules every 15 minutes, it tracks ρ̂ (the busiest person's committed time) live, and when no valid schedule exists *"the season is declared collapsed and archived."* Presented as a living preprint, complete with footnotes and a nod to Fourier's phalanxes. The line that makes it art rather than a demo: *"The polycule grows on guilt."*
  - *Riff:* the honest one is that it is a satire of exactly the product I just proposed above, and worth reading before building it. Its joke is that the optimiser never asks anyone what they want. Any real version has to make the preferences and the weights the editable, visible, negotiated part, and the solver the boring bit underneath. That is the difference between a tool and a rota.

**Below the headline bar, noted:** **ha-buttplug** (DevelopmentalOctopus, 30 stars) puts buttplug devices into **Home Assistant** as entities via HACS, which makes intimate hardware automatable from the same stack that runs the lights; real but rough, 16 commits, the author concedes *"lots more work to be done handling error cases"*, a server disconnect needs an integration reset, and it cannot cope with two devices of the same name, so treat it as a proof of concept rather than something to build on (https://github.com/DevelopmentalOctopus/ha-buttplug). **LilaZap** (LilaHexe, Codeberg), a *tension-activated* shock collar, i.e. predicament engineering as a product, which is the open lane I flagged on 22 Sep; only noting it, and anything in this class needs the usual discipline (hard output ceiling in firmware, below the waist, both electrodes on the same side, nothing near the heart, independent mechanical release, and a person present). On the **competitor-intel** side the D/s app cluster has grown since September: **EverCollar** (evercollar.com, iOS and Android) adds consent-framed GPS location sharing to the tasks-and-rituals formula, and its one genuinely good design decision is that the tracked person can disable sharing unilaterally without the partner's approval, with status permanently visible; **Bonded** (bonded.partners, web only, native apps pending) is the kitchen-sink entrant, bundling rules-with-evidence, limits negotiation, chastity, budget, diary, timeline and files.   - The sibling find: **ESP32-DATV** (SP8ESA), which uses the ESP32-C3's own 2.4GHz radio to broadcast **DVB-S2 digital television**. A three-quid microcontroller as a standalone digital TV beacon, no RF front end. https://github.com/SP8ESA/ESP32-DATV (write-up https://hackaday.com/2026/10/14/using-an-esp32-c3s-rf-hardware-for-dvb-s2-video-transmission/)
  - *Riff:* every ESP32 in this house is now also a 2.4GHz spectrum analyser. That is the honest diagnostic for the weak-2.4GHz wedging problem (the LG TV and the bedroom Smart Clock going deaf while still associated): instead of inferring congestion from UniFi's client view, park a spare ESP32 near the TV and actually look at the band. Receive-only, costs nothing, answers a question we have been guessing at. Transmit is a licensing question, not a hobby one, so leave that side alone.
- **esp32sim** (mikroverk, MIT, 213 stars): an ESP32 emulator in Rust that is not a toy. Instruction-level emulation of four SoCs across **both** Espressif architectures (Xtensa for ESP32/S3, RISC-V for C3/C6), each booting the **real mask ROM**, the real second-stage bootloader and an unmodified application image. No patched firmware, no stubs. Enough of the SoC is modelled to run real projects end to end: GPIO, UART, timers, SPI displays (ST7789, ST7735, ST7701S with touch), WS2812, camera, audio, board profiles like the TTGO T-Display. Decoder validated against full instruction listings (977k instructions, zero mismatches on the S3). Runs locally or in the browser. https://github.com/mikroverk/esp32sim
  - *Riff:* this is the answer to the saline-pump development loop. Firmware changes currently mean an OTA to the board at .126 and a physical rig to watch, which is exactly the setup that makes starting a session feel expensive. Emulated, the state machine, the UI and the timing logic can be wrung out with no board, no pump, no saline and nothing to waste. It will not model the peristaltic heads or the driver current, so the safety-critical end still gets tested on the bench, but everything above that layer stops needing hardware.
- **SolidCraft** (tangency-labs, Apache-2.0, **4,574 stars in eleven days**): a real parametric CAD application written in pure Rust. Sketch, constrain, feature timeline, change a dimension and the model rebuilds. Desktop (Windows/macOS/Linux/Snap), in the browser via WASM, and, the part that matters here, **a built-in MCP server so an agent can model directly**. https://github.com/tangency-labs/solidcraft (site https://solidcraft.io, browser app https://solidcraft.io/app)
  - Note the URL: the repo moved from `bherbruck/solidcraft` to `tangency-labs/solidcraft`. The old path redirects, cite the new one.
  - *Riff:* this closes the gap the Huer mount job exposed. Right now CAD here is either build123d in a venv (I can drive it, Rob cannot open the result in anything he uses) or a Fusion API script (Rob has to run it on the Mac, and when it came out wrong neither of us could see why). - **Halcyon Video** (GPL-3.0, 930 stars): your Jellyfin, Plex or Emby library redrawn as a **walkable 1990s video rental shop** in the browser. Real 3D aisles under strip lights, every film a case on a shelf, pull one down, turn it over and the specs printed on the back are the actual MediaStreams metadata Jellyfin already holds. Carry it to the counter to start playback. It keeps no library of its own, reads the server over the API, and reports start/progress/stop back so resume points and watch history stay correct. Sections come from your libraries and genres, sequels group from BoxSets, RomM adds a games department, Jellyseerr lets you request what is not on the shelf.   - *Riff:* the actual idea worth stealing is that **browsing is not search**. A poster grid optimises for finding the thing you already decided on; a shelf optimises for the thing you did not know you wanted. That is a UI argument, not a nostalgia one, and it applies to anything with a catalogue. For the house it is simply a good Friday night with Dexter and Logan.

**Meta this week:** three of the eight are the same move, which is *the capability was already in the hardware and the vendor just did not expose it*. ### DIY mad-scientist kink hardware

- **k250-forge** (vybesforge, Sep 2026, Apache-2.0, Python). A reverse-engineered BLE driver and pattern engine for the Kink K250-4S four-channel e-stim box, every byte measured against real hardware with the box's own LCD as the witness. That part is good work. The part that makes this the find of the week is the threat model: the README states plainly that an AI driving an e-stim box is a genuinely new failure mode, and that the risk is not malice but **drift**, a model that takes a "more" from one turn and carries it into the next until a percentage has crept up while nobody was watching. So the ceiling is not in a prompt, it is in the code. `power.max_percent` in a human-owned `limits.json` is the contract; a command-line `--hardcap` can only ever *lower* it and prints when it clamps. Three separate ceilings (power, frequency, slew rate on the dial), each settable per channel because different channels sit on different skin and one global ceiling is the wrong shape. A session ledger refuses to start once the time budget is spent, whoever is asking. And the doc is explicit that the hardware kill switch (hold any knob two seconds) is the real backstop, needing no software, no network and no AI in the loop. Safety framing is theirs and it is correct: never a pad path across the chest or through the heart, never on skin that cannot feel it, loose loops because circulation at the electrode is the real injury risk, and "they feel nothing" is a fault to investigate at lower power, not a reason to turn up. https://github.com/vybesforge/k250-forge
  - *We could riff on this by:* this is Tethered's thesis expressed as a device driver. A limits file the sub owns, that the dom's tooling can only tighten, with every clamp logged. Tethered currently has consent as text; this has consent as an enforced ceiling with an audit trail. A "negotiated limits" object that hardware drivers can read is a product, and nobody has built the registry.

- **OpenValence** (new org, Oct 2026). Not a project, an ecosystem, and it is what the OSSM world has been missing. **Valence** is a hub-and-spoke protocol spec for linear motion machines with a registry and reference clients (MIT). **Nucleus** is the hub firmware for the new OSSM Flagship board, pure ESP-IDF on an ESP32-P4 plus ESP32-C6, reading quadrature off the low-power core. **Hardware** is the Flagship PCB in KiCad under CERN-OHL-S. **Phosphor** is the cross-platform client. They even maintain a buttplug.io fork carrying a Valence hardware manager. The whole stack went public this month and is moving daily. https://openvalence.org/ | https://github.com/openvalence
  - *We could riff on this by:* **Isotope** is the one to watch. It is the accessory firmware for the ESP-NOW network around the hub, and the design is openly ESPHome-shaped: you describe an accessory in a declarative config, a generator turns it into a flashable image, the accessory announces its own channels to the hub when it joins, and the hub (never the client) runs the relationships. Every accessory fails safe alone: no hub beacon for five seconds, or a goodbye, and each actuator goes to its declared safe value. The two example accessories named in the design are a motorized stand and **a peristaltic pump**. That is the saline rig, described by someone else, as a channel on a standard bus. Nothing runs yet, it is a design home with a validation board, which means the config format is still arguable and we are early enough to argue. https://github.com/openvalence/Isotope

- **Kinetic** (openvalence, Apache-2.0, header-only C++20). The motion planner underneath all of the above, and it stands alone: no hardware dependencies, builds native, ESP32-P4 and WebAssembly, with a browser playground. You hand it knots ("be at this position at this time") and it returns a sampleable trajectory that never exceeds the velocity, acceleration and jerk limits you set, as a composite cubic Bezier in the time-position plane, so position and velocity are continuous by construction and the motor never sees a step in speed. Two design decisions are worth stealing outright. First, **time never gives, amplitude does**: a knot the limits cannot reach in time moves toward the knot before it rather than being dropped or arriving late, so a script keeps its rhythm and surrenders stroke length instead. Second, **limits are ceilings, not targets**, and every trim, clamp and refusal is recorded as a readable anomaly, so you can see exactly where a pattern asked for too much. https://github.com/openvalence/Kinetic
  - *We could riff on this by:* Fuck-io's hard problem was always "arbitrary script in, motor-safe curve out" and this is that problem solved, header-only, by people who build the machines. Drop it in rather than writing a ramp generator.

- **espgasm / Pro-Edge** (jabiim, Jul 2026, ESP32 / PlatformIO). Someone already built the orgasm-detect plug. An inflatable plug's internal pressure goes to an ADC pin through a trimpot-gain amplifier, the firmware watches for the pressure rise that precedes climax, and a mode state machine (Denial and Release goals, edge variance, drop styles, break timers) throttles or cuts the toy to run an edging session. The architectural choice is the interesting bit: the board has **no PWM output pin and drives no motor**. It is sensor, brain and UI only, and streams the computed speed over a WebSocket to a browser page that relays it to Buttplug.io, so the toy can be any supported device wherever the Bluetooth dongle lives. Lineage is night-howler's Protogasm and jwgscott's Pro-Edge. https://github.com/jabiim/espgasm
  - *We could riff on this by:* separating sensing from actuation across a WebSocket is exactly the split Fuck-io wants, and it means the detect-plug and the stepper machine never need to be the same board or even the same room. Safety-wise, the no-output design is also the fail-safe one: a crashed sensor board cannot leave a motor running.
