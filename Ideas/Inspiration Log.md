### The gaps (what the community actually says)

1. The incumbent is **Google Calendar plus a Google Doc**. The doc holds the agreements (no history, no sign-off, no notifications), the calendar holds the dates (all-or-nothing sharing). Both wrong for the job.
2. **Scheduling is named the #1 pain, above jealousy** (a Sept 2025 podcast is literally titled "Polyamory's Biggest Struggle Isn't Jealousy, It's Scheduling"). Yet pure calendars keep dying because Google is good enough at calendaring. The Poly Life (2014) is the canonical corpse; the community now *satirises* the category (Just Poly Everything was an April Fools app whose punchline was AI polycule scheduling).
3. **Agreements are "living documents" in every essay and static docs in every real polycule.** No versioning, no "who agreed to what, when", no review reminders. Nothing exists.
4. **Sexual-health coordination is spreadsheets and trust-chain disclosure.** Nobody models the network (metamour's test lapses = your risk picture changed).
5. **NRE / jealousy / feelings tracking: zero tooling** despite endless essays.
6. **Privacy is existential** (outing risk: custody, jobs). Self-hosted or E2EE is a real differentiator for the tech-literate slice, which is a big slice.

### Rob-could-build-this, ranked

1. **Git for relationship agreements.** Structured agreements with full history, diffs, per-person sign-off on every change, expiry/reaffirmation dates, RADAR-style check-ins that produce commits. Nobody has built it, and it's Tethered's DNA (consent, accountability between intimate partners) pointed at ENM. Fleshed out same day (16:11-16:32): the moat is clause-level everything (visibility, ratification, history), not document-level, since Google Docs already does edit-history but not sign-off; mechanics are four verbs (propose, ratify, live, review); calendar comes back in as a per-clause audience view. **Parked by Rob 16:32**, same reasoning as Tethered: sound analysis, same distribution problem (niche, bought after a rupture, no way to reach users). His brief now: build what's fun first, treat money as a bonus, hence the Fuck-io steer that day (see `Projects/Fuck-io.md`, `Daily/2026-09-22.md`).
2. **The operational polycule graph.** Make polycul.es DO something: nodes carry testing dates and agreement links, edges carry relationship type and cadence; the graph drives calendar-sharing scopes and propagates safer-sex status changes along edges. Every existing tool is a static picture.
3. **Calendar fairness analytics.** A layer over existing calendars (CalDAV/Google), not another calendar: time distribution per partner, NRE skew detection ("new connection is eating 70% of your free evenings"), "you haven't had a solo night in 3 weeks". Tide's mood/journal plumbing is half of this already.
4. **Audience-scoped household calendar.** The same evening renders differently to kids ("Dad's out"), nesting partner (full detail), metamour (busy/free). PYE does per-partner privacy but has no concept of a household or kids. Haven/Tide already solve per-person visibility walls; this is the problem nobody else even sees, and it is literally this household's lived problem.
5. **UK testing-chain coordinator.** Cadence per the network's protocol, reminders around new-partner events, per-edge private attestations, "risk picture changed upstream" alerts without naming names. Highest trust burden, hence last.

**Meta-lesson from the graveyard:** poly *calendars* die (Google wins at calendaring). What survives is poly-native *data models*: consent (PYE), agreements-as-profiles (Nymph), analytics (DaterGraph), events (Plura). Model the things Google structurally cannot: agreements, audiences, edges, history.

## 2026-09-22 (addendum 2: FOC-Stim v4 cost estimate, Rob's request)

Sources: repo BOM docs (`docs/focstim-v4-BOM.md`) + production files (`schematics/V4/BOM_FOC-Stim-v4.3_mainboard.csv`, which carries LCSC unit prices).

**What v4 buys over v1:** 4 outputs (v1 has 3), battery (7h on a 2000mAh), WiFi (ESP32-S3), OLED + volume knob, accelerometer, 3D-printed case. Flashing is all USB (webflasher for the ESP32, Restim's firmware updater for the STM32G473), no programmer needed.

**Cost breakdown for one working unit:**

| Chunk | Est (landed, GBP) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| JLCPCB mainboard PCBA | £110-145 | Parts on the board sum to ~$28.50/board at LCSC prices (STM32G473 $3.66, ESP32-S3-MINI $4.63, 4x DRV8231A $6.35, fuel gauge + charger + boost ~$4.2). Min assembly qty is 2, plus setup ~$8, extended-part loading fees (~15+ types x $3 = the silent killer), 5 bare PCBs, shipping + 20% VAT. You end up with a spare assembled board. |
| Frontpanel PCB | £5-15 | Bare from JLC in the same order, hand-solder the SMD nuts/headers (or pay JLC another setup fee to assemble). |
| 4x Xicon 42TL004-RC transformers | £20-30 Mouser, £10-15 eBay knockoff | Hand-soldered, not part of the PCBA. Retroamplis has them at EUR 6.95 each as a sanity check; couldn't get a live Mouser quote (JS-walled). |
| Aliexpress basket | £30-40 | OLED display ~£2.50, 4x Amass 2mm banana jacks, encoder (~£1 LCSC), knob, heatset inserts, feet, JST battery connector, 103450 3-pin JST-PH battery ~£8-12, 2mm banana cables + 2mm-to-4mm adapters. |
| Case | ~£0-20 | 3D printed (STLs in repo). Pennies in filament on our own printer, ~£15-20 printed by a service. |

**Total: realistically £170-230 landed**, frugal floor ~£150 (eBay transformers, no battery, bare frontpanel), and the JLC minimums mean a spare assembled mainboard + 3 spare bare boards fall out of it. Second unit would cost ~£60-80 in bits.

**Context:** v1 route (B-G431B-ESC1 dev board + transformer output stage) is ~£50-70 all-in but 3 outputs, mains-tethered, no display/battery/WiFi. The v4 is roughly 3x the money for the actual-product experience. A commercial 4-channel current-controlled box does not exist to compare against; the nearest (ET312 clones, Coyote 3) are neither current-controlled nor spatially steerable.

**Hand-assembly addendum (Rob asked, 22 Sep):** checked the v4.3 mainboard BOM footprints. ~55 distinct parts, ~115 placements, passives down to 0402, and five leadless/near-leadless packages: 4x DRV8231 (WSON-8, thermal pad), BQ24075 fuel gauge (QFN-16, thermal pad), TPS61170 boost (QFN-6), LSM6DSOX accelerometer (LGA-14, pads fully underneath), TPS631000 (SOT-583, 0.5mm pitch). The STM32 is LQFP-64 (leaded, drag-solderable). Verdict: not an iron job; needs stencil + solder paste + hotplate or hot air. DIY route: 5 bare PCBs + stencil from JLC ~£20-25, LCSC parts ~£30-35/board, hotplate ~£35 if not owned. Saves ~£50-70 vs PCBA, before any scrapped chips. Fine as a deliberate learn-SMD-reflow project; false economy otherwise. The hand-soldering that's *meant* to be done by the builder: transformers, jacks, encoder, battery connector, frontpanel.

**Through-hole redesign addendum (Rob asked, 22 Sep):** a true THT redesign is impossible, not just hard. None of the load-bearing silicon exists in through-hole packages: no DIP STM32G4 (or any modern 32-bit STM32), no DIP ESP32-S3, no DIP H-bridge with the DRV8231's current-sense/regulation behaviour, and the battery fuel gauge/boost/accelerometer are SMD-only categories. DIP microcontrollers stopped at roughly the ATmega328 generation, an order of magnitude short of the G473's job (4x simultaneous current-loop control at motor-drive PWM rates). The honest THT-equivalent path is a *module carrier*: WeAct STM32G4 core board + ESP32-S3 devkit + driver breakout modules on a big 2.54mm-pitch carrier PCB. Buildable with an iron, but it's a fork, not a build: new schematic, new pin mapping, firmware port, re-validation of a device that puts current through a human, and it lands £60-90 in modules for a bulkier, less safe result than £110-145 of JLC PCBA. And the v1 route already IS the module approach done properly: the B-G431B-ESC1 dev board is the pre-assembled SMD heart, builder adds the through-hole output stage (transformers, connectors) by hand. Conclusion: "through-hole FOC-Stim" exists and it's called v1.

### Wildcard

- **Fugleramme** (arnegiacomo, 2390 points on HN, 15 Sep): a picture frame that LISTENS to your garden. BirdNET-Go runs fully local on a Pi 5, identifies birds by song through a mic, and the frame renders whatever is currently out there as hand-cut 1800s natural-history plates on a 13.3" Inky Impression e-ink panel. Over 800 curated public-domain cutouts covering 400+ species, sized by body mass, larger birds drawn toward the centre, empty frame shows a bare perch. No camera, no cloud, no screen-glow. The thing that makes it art rather than a gadget is the restraint: it never shows you data, it shows you a Victorian engraving that happens to be true right now. E-ink is optional, it will render to any HDMI display or over the network. https://github.com/arnegiacomo/fugleramme
  - *Riff:* the pattern generalises to "local classifier plus a curated art vocabulary, rendered as a still object." Same shape would work for a Tide/DAKboard ambient panel that draws the house state rather than dashboards it.
- **OpenLaneLink** (Nicholas Hall, 2935 points, write-up Aug 2026): a power surge killed his bowling alley's proprietary scoring system, quote to replace was $120k, so he rebuilt the lot for about $1,600 in ESP32s. Gateway nodes, pinsetter interface controllers, pin/foul/ball-speed sensors. The bit worth stealing is the architecture, not the saving: every node emits the SAME 72-byte struct over EITHER transport, ESPNow wireless mesh or RS-485 wired, so the two paths are redundant rather than layered, and a lane pair keeps scoring through a total server outage. One message shape, two transports, graceful degradation by default. Open source. https://nicholas-a-hall.github.io/openlanelink/ (discussion: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48968606)
  - *Riff:* directly applicable to anything with an ESP32 and a session that must not die mid-use. Same-struct-two-transports is the honest answer to "what happens when the WiFi drops during a scene."
- **Daisy** (Jude Robinson, student final project, Hackaday 24 Sep): a machine that makes literal daisy chains. Two opposing X-Y gantries with grippers, a conveyor of daisies, a scalpel blade and a threading post; the gantries alternate roles, one holding the chain while the other threads, slits and adds the next flower. The reason it is in here is the design lesson: handling naturally variable plant material should need adaptive sensing, and it needs none, because V-profile grippers self-centre any stem diameter and the blade sheath constrains the stem while it is pierced. An uncertain biological problem converted into a deterministic mechanical one by geometry alone. https://judeotis.me/projects/Daisy/
  - *Riff:* the standing correction to how we think about the stepper machine and the saline rig. Before adding a sensor and a control loop, ask what shape would make the sensor unnecessary. Cheaper, and it fails predictably.

### Household / self-hosted with a clever core

- **ESPectre** (francescopace, 9.4k stars, very active): occupancy sensing on a bare ESP32 with NO sensor attached. It reads WiFi channel state information, computes spatial turbulence (standard deviation across subcarriers) and tracks its variance; a body moving through the room produces characteristic variance spikes distinguishable from noise. Works on ESP32, S2, S3, C3, C5, C6, needs only a 2.4GHz 802.11n network. Ships MQTT with Home Assistant discovery, an ESPHome path for native entities, and Matter as a standard occupancy sensor. Author is explicit about the limits: it detects radio-environment change, it does not identify or count people, does not prove a room is empty, and is not a safety-certified system. https://github.com/francescopace/espectre
  - *Riff:* the ESP32s already scattered round this house become presence sensors for free, no PIR, no mmWave, no new hardware, nothing pointed at anyone. Whole-house presence for the cost of flashing what we own.
- **wifisense-pi** (The-Masked-Bear, Apache-2.0, archived read-only 6 Sep 2026): the same CSI trick taken to its logical end. ESP32-S3 samples the radio channel 100 times a second, a Pi 4 does the DSP, and it resolves respiration rate at 8-36 breaths/min validated to plus or minus 0.1 bpm, through plasterboard, wood and glass. Refreshingly honest README: cannot locate people without multiple nodes, cannot count them, cannot identify anyone, cannot do sleep stages, and explicitly separates itself from the MIT RF-Pose demos that used purpose-built FMCW radar rather than commodity WiFi. "A bedroom instrument built from a $5 microcontroller." https://github.com/The-Masked-Bear/wifisense-pi
  - *Riff:* the missing input for the scene-telemetry dashboard idea from 22 Sep. Contactless vitals means no chest strap, no wires on the bottom, nothing to snag. Monitoring only, and only ever as an extra signal alongside a human paying attention, never as a safety mechanism in its own right. Archived, so it is a reference design to learn CSI DSP from, not a dependency.
- **lossless-memory** (aru-labs, Show HN 21 Sep): a personal AI memory that refuses to summarise, on the argument that compression is where assistants quietly lose you. Three mechanisms: every conversation turn kept raw in JSONL (timestamp, actor, role, type, text, model, session); TIME as the primary search axis, so "last Tuesday night" is parsed into a range and used to restrict the candidate set BEFORE any ranking happens rather than hoping embeddings surface it; and an "LLL index", a tiny where-are-we-now topic marker reinjected every turn so it survives context compaction and session boundaries. SQLite FTS5 plus sqlite-vec, local sentence-transformers as a fallback rather than the main path, re-index daemon every 10 minutes. **Meta this week:** the through-line across ESPectre, wifisense-pi and Daisy is *sense less, infer more*. Two of them delete the sensor by reading a signal that was already in the room; the third deletes it by choosing a shape that cannot go wrong. All three are the opposite of the usual instinct, which is to bolt on another module.
