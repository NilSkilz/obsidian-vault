**Source:** WhatsApp export (no media), 2 Jul 2025 to 8 Sep 2026 09:34. 9,761 messages. Raw file lives OUTSIDE the vault at `/home/jarvis/whatsapp-aimee/_chat.txt` (never committed). Analysis by Jarvis, 8 Sep 2026, at Rob's request during the post-row processing.

## The numbers

- **Volume is balanced:** Rob 5,022 / Aimee 4,739 messages (51/49). Similar message lengths (median ~35-38 chars both).
- **Rob carries the channel's heartbeat:** he sends the first message of the day 277 times to her 135 (2:1), and restarts conversations after a 3h+ gap 408 to 221. The daily good-morning "love u baby" is his ritual, near-unbroken for 14 months.
- **Both reply fast:** median reply latency 4-5 minutes both directions. Neither ghosts. Silence beyond a couple of hours is a genuine outlier in this dataset.
- **Affection density is high both ways:** "love u/you" appears 735 times from Rob, 556 from Aimee. She matches him; he leads.
- **Aug 2026 is Rob's most one-sided month** (ratio 1.36, his highest ever). As the Mary period ramped up, he was doing measurably more of the reaching out.

## Finding 1: WhatsApp is the warm channel. The rows happen off it.

7 Sep 2026 is the cleanest example: playful, affectionate messages all day (dog photos, "just a cuddle when ur home 🥰"), then the 1am row happened entirely off-WhatsApp. Same for most conflicts: the text record holds the *aftermath and repair*, not the fight. Consequence: "who said what" disputes about verbal conversations mostly can't be settled from this record, which is exactly the failure mode the comms log was built for.

## Finding 2: The Oxford premise IS in writing, and Aimee recited it herself

This corrects the earlier record ("said verbally but unlogged"). It's better than that:

- **Aimee, 1 Sep 2026 10:50:** *"we had only just had a chat where you said you weren't going to do one off's and were going to focus on looking for a long term thing to replace Tash. **Ideally someone who isn't going to pop up everywhere and cause a conflict at events etc.**"* She stated Rob's design premise, in her own words, attributing it to him, seven days before "you're springing this on me."

So the premise landed, was understood, and was repeated back. Last night's "she IS part of the local scene now" is a widening of her own written position from a week earlier, not a discovery of new information.

Rob's improvements (advance notice, headlines, the comms log) have all been on the decision side. 1 Oct 2025, Rob: *"I genuinely thought you'd be happy with me today - talking to you about it days before leaving so it wasn't last minute"* — he keeps optimising notice; she keeps asking for inclusion. That's the precise gap that never closes.

## Finding 6: Rob's care claim is fully documented; the gap is elsewhere

The record shows: daily affection, constant check-ins, "is there anything I can do to reassure you?" as a recurring refrain across the whole 14 months, repeated unprompted offers to cancel or stop seeing Tash entirely (21 Aug 2026, 1 Sep 2026), lunches, planned treats after her bad days. Nobody reading this chat could conclude "doesn't provide care." What the record also shows is that reassurance-on-demand has been the primary tool for 14 months and the underlying insecurity is unchanged, which matches what the research on reassurance loops predicts: it soothes the moment and feeds the cycle.

## Finding 7: The anxiety is bidirectional

Rob in writing: *"I'm in a full spiral and not coping well"* (22 Jul 2025), panic attack in the car (18 Jun 2026), *"I thought u were mad at me and I didn't know why. Then I just spiralled"* (26 Jul 2026), *"I feel like I'm in trouble or have done something wrong... I don't know if I'm just imagining it"* (4 Aug 2026). Aimee catches him well in those moments, same as he catches her. This is two anxious people taking turns, not a stable partner managing an anxious one. Worth remembering when "why am I even bothering" hits: the record shows her doing the same holding work in his lows.

## Finding 8: Repair is fast and neither stonewalls

Written rows resolve in hours, a consistent shape: Rob offers something concrete (cuddle, lunch, cancel, a treat day), Aimee softens with an apology for the "freak out", warmth resumes same day. E.g. 21 Aug 2026: full panic at 12:24, "Love you too. Sorry I'm having a freak out" by 12:29, circus planning by 13:31. Against this baseline the current silence (no reply to 8 Sep 09:34 as of ~noon) is unusual, but the baseline contains almost no post-major-row mornings, so it's an outlier without being a verdict.

Conversations that matter should happen in that window, not at 1am, and arguably partly *in writing*, where her decoded voice demonstrably lives.
5. **The comparison wound is the real target** and it's 13+ months old (Finding 5). No Mary-specific rule can fix a pre-Mary wound. The feed-first promise and public-conduct agreement (8 Sep chat) treat the visibility symptom; the wound itself is the long game of aimed desire, already discussed.
