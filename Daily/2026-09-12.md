### 17:55 Saline Pump: Stage 7f, the glass lights up (and the first remote flash)
Rob flashed 7e and it booted clean: `alive 10s  heap 211640  wifi STA  192.168.1.126  screens FAILED`. No hang, WiFi up, phone UI serving. The board being on the LAN meant I could query `/status` from here instead of guessing: `screens:0, scomp:1`, so the glass was compiled in and the frame buffer was the only thing that failed. Rob's point that `bringup.ino` drives the same panels fine confirmed it, that sketch draws straight to the TFT and never asks for a buffer.

**The fault:** a 240x240x16 face is 115200 bytes in one contiguous lump. The board reports plenty free but its **largest single block is 98292**. Never a leak, never the wiring, and no amount of freeing heap would have fixed it. This is also almost certainly the original "dead board" from earlier in the week, back when a failed alloc halted `setup()` in `while(true)`.

**The fix:** `BandCanvas`, a `GFXcanvas16` subclass that presents itself to Adafruit_GFX as a full 240x240 surface but only owns `bandH` rows. The face is rendered once per band with everything outside clipped, and each band is pushed to its own address window. Every GFX primitive funnels through `drawPixel` / `drawFastVLine` / `drawFastHLine` / `fillScreen`, so overriding those four was the entire job: **not one line of the drawing code changed**. Band height is chosen at boot, biggest the heap will take, from 240/120/80/60/48/40/30/24. This board landed on 120, so two bands of 57600 bytes.

**Flashed it myself over the air** (espota to 192.168.1.126, pumps forced off on OTA start, no 12V connected anyway). First attempt died at 39% with a broken pipe, second went through. Verified on the live board, not just compiled: `screens:1, band:120, heap:146604, maxblk:98292`, and `/status` answering in 12-59ms with both faces drawing, so 7d's responsiveness survived the glass coming on.

`/status` now also reports band height, free heap and largest block, so this class of fault is diagnosable from the phone. Serial heartbeat prints `heap free/largest` for the same reason.

### 18:05 Saline Pump: Stage 7g, knobs on (the rig is complete)
Last subsystem. `ENABLE_KNOBS 1`, flashed over the air, both KY-040s armed on the live board and confirmed `enc:1` / `enc:1`. The steadiness probe passed on GPIO34/35 with the encoders actually plugged in (their own 10k pull-ups hold those input-only pins steady), and `/status` kept answering in 10-47ms under a sustained hammer, so no interrupt storm and no starved `loop()`.

- **Fixed the one real hole in the boot-safety story first.** The runaway-edge guard from 7a lived in `loop()`, and `loop()` starving is the exact symptom it exists to catch: a pin chattering flat out means the guard never gets to run. It is a dead-man's switch now. `encStormGuard()` zeroes the edge counter once a second; if the ISR sees that counter pass the storm ceiling it knows `loop()` has not run for a second, and it disables its own two pins from inside the interrupt with a bare `GPIO_PINn_REG` write (no `detachInterrupt`, no gpio driver call, both flash-resident and illegal from an ISR). `loop()` revives, finds the over-threshold count, and does the flash-side tidy-up. Four layers, and the deepest no longer depends on what it protects.
- Controls are as designed: turn = 1% per detent (same variable as the phone slider), short press = stop that side, hold 1.2s = start.
- 83% flash. `encISR` confirmed in IRAM, `QTAB` in DRAM. Page re-verified in headless Chrome **against the real board rather than a mock**: no JS errors, BENCH MODE banner gone, KNOBS card live and both reading ON. Screenshots at `/tmp/pump7g-*.png`.

## Decisions & Notes
- New lesson filed: **free heap is not the number that matters, largest contiguous block is.** Two sessions chased a leak that did not exist because the heartbeat only printed free heap. Always print both. Big single buffers are a design smell on the ESP32; band-render instead, and pick the band size at runtime.
- The board is reachable from CT 110 and OTA works, so I can flash and verify the pump firmware myself. No more "compiles clean but nobody has run it" handoffs.
- New lesson: **a safety guard must not live inside the thing it protects.** The floating-pin guard sat in `loop()` for two stages, and the failure mode it catches is precisely `loop()` never running. Generalises: watchdogs belong strictly below the level they watch.
