# Scorpion Rig — Kee Klamp Buy List

Consolidated hardware order for the DIY DeviceBondage "scorpion" rig (kneeling bench + Kee Klamp armature). Frame fittings plus the load-bearing base hardware in one list so it ships as a single order. See [[reference_scorpion_device]] for the build context and the e-stim safety notes.

## Frame — Kee Klamp + pipe

- Kee Klamp fittings to match the armature design (elbows, tees, single/two-socket cross-overs, swivels for the adjustable arms) — size to the pipe below.
- Galvanised steel pipe uprights + arms, cut to length. **Steel, not ally** for anything carrying the cantilevered head.
- Pipe base flanges (the disc bases) — one per leg.

## Base hardware — the load-bearing bit (the part that actually holds a person)

**Do NOT use top-side screw-in threaded inserts for the load flanges.** The cantilevered forward blocks put those flanges into tension/prying (uplift), and screw-in inserts in plywood fail exactly in tension — the insert tears out and ply wallows around it under dynamic cycling. Tightness checks won't catch a slow crush before it lets go.

Two acceptable routes, both "bolt into metal, not wood":

**Option A — T-nuts from below (preferred)**
- Pronged (tee) T-nuts, driven up from the underside of the ply. Captive, prongs bite, load spreads across the flange = far higher pull-out.
- Counterbore a shallow pocket on the underside so the T-nut sits flush.

**Option B — through-bolt + nyloc (strongest)**
- Full through-bolts, penny/repair washer + nyloc underneath.
- Counterbore a pocket in the underside of the ply so nut + washer sink flush.

## Common to both

- **Bolt size: M8 minimum** for the load flanges AND the pad blocks. No M6 on anything structural.
- **Blue threadlock (Loctite 243)** on every bolt — no nyloc option top-side, and you want it even with T-nuts.
- **Counterbore:** shallow pocket on the underside sized to sink the T-nut (Option A) or nut + repair washer (Option B) flush, so nothing sits proud under the mat.
- **Rubber anti-slip mat** over the top. It handles shear (sideways) only — it is NOT holding the rig down. Uplift is on the bolts.

## Safety carry-overs (from the rig notes, not the order)

- Swap **bare copper** e-stim contacts for **stainless** — copper corrodes and pinpoints current (burns/marks).
- Keep the belly contact **low, below the navel**, so the circuit stays below the chest.

Tightness checks are now the backup, not the only line of defence — which is where you want them on something holding a person.
